🔎 How to Spot a Scam Website (12 Checks)

  1. Check domain age with a WHOIS lookup — most fake stores are under 90 days old.
  2. Look for a real address and phone number — then verify them on a map.
  3. Search ‘[site name] + scam’ and ‘[site name] + reviews’ on independent sites.
  4. Be suspicious of 60–90% discounts on in-demand items.
  5. Check whether the text is copied from another store (search a sentence in quotes).
  6. Only pay by credit card or a protected payment method — never wire, crypto or gift cards.
  7. Look for odd grammar, mismatched currencies, and stock photos of ‘staff’.
  8. Check social links actually lead to active, older profiles.
  9. Check the return policy: vague, missing, or ‘ship back to overseas address at your cost’ is a flag.
  10. Beware of countdown timers and fake ‘X people are viewing’ pop-ups.
  11. A padlock only means the connection is encrypted — not that the seller is honest.
  12. Use our Link Red-Flag Scanner and Shame-O-Meter before paying.

🚨 Scammed? The First 24 Hours Action Plan

  1. Hour 0: call your bank / card issuer; block the card, request a chargeback or wire recall.
  2. Hour 1: change passwords (email first), enable 2FA, sign out of all devices.
  3. Hour 2: screenshot everything — URLs, chats, profiles, receipts, numbers.
  4. Hour 3: report to your national fraud agency and get a reference number.
  5. Hour 4: if ID documents were shared, freeze credit and place fraud alerts.
  6. Day 1: warn friends/family if your accounts were used to message them.
  7. Day 1: request a free case review on ShameOnYou.com.
  8. Ignore anyone offering to ‘recover’ money for an upfront fee.

📉 Shrinkflation: How to Catch It and Fight Back

  1. Always compare the unit price (per 100 g, per oz, per sheet) — not the shelf price.
  2. Photograph the pack size of products you buy often; compare over time.
  3. Watch for ‘new look’, ‘improved recipe’ or ‘new size’ labels.
  4. Check sheet counts, ply and item counts on paper and household goods.
  5. Switch to brands or store labels that kept their size.
  6. Log your catches on our Shrinkflation Tracker contest for a chance to win.

✂️ How to Cancel Any Subscription (Scripts Included)

  1. Find the cancel path: Account → Billing → Manage plan. Search ‘[service] cancel’ if hidden.
  2. Cancel in writing whenever possible and screenshot the confirmation.
  3. Script: “I am cancelling my subscription effective immediately. Please confirm in writing and do not charge my card again.”
  4. If they refuse: ask for a supervisor, note the name, date and time.
  5. Remove your card from the account and use your app store / card issuer to block renewals.
  6. Dispute any charge after cancellation with your card issuer — attach your proof.

🕳️ Dark Patterns: The 12 Tricks Apps Play on You

  1. Roach motel — easy in, hard out.
  2. Confirmshaming — guilt-trip opt-out text.
  3. Sneak into basket — extras added without asking.
  4. Hidden costs — fees revealed at the last step.
  5. Forced continuity — free trial silently converts to paid.
  6. Disguised ads — ads styled as content or buttons.
  7. Trick questions — confusing double negatives in consent boxes.
  8. Fake urgency — countdowns that reset.
  9. Fake scarcity — ‘only 1 left’ that never runs out.
  10. Nagging — repeated prompts until you give in.
  11. Privacy zuckering — pre-ticked data sharing.
  12. Bait and switch — you click for one thing, get another.

✍️ Write a Complaint Companies Can’t Ignore

  1. Lead with the outcome you want (refund of $X, repair, cancellation).
  2. State the facts: dates, order numbers, amounts — no insults.
  3. Reference their own policy or advertised promise.
  4. Give a deadline (usually 14 days).
  5. Say what you’ll do next: card dispute, regulator, small-claims court.
  6. Send it to executive customer relations, not the generic inbox.
  7. Keep copies of everything. Escalate calmly and on schedule.
